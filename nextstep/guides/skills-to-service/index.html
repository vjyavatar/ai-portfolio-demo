THE NEXTSTEP FIELD GUIDE

Turn everyday skills into a small service

A useful service starts with something another person finds difficult. You do not need to master every new AI tool before you can help.

Start with evidence of a skill

List three tasks people already ask you to help with. Perhaps you explain schoolwork patiently, make confusing documents readable, organize schedules, or troubleshoot a spreadsheet. Choose a task you can demonstrate today.

Describe one person and one problem

“I help everyone with AI” is difficult to evaluate. “I help a local club organize its volunteer schedule” gives someone a clear reason to talk to you. Write the person, their task, and what currently goes wrong.

Make the offer small enough to finish

Choose one deliverable, a clear boundary, and a review step. An example: one event checklist with owners and deadlines, reviewed with the organizer. Agree on the scope, timing, and any price before starting. Avoid ongoing support promises in an initial experiment.

Build a sample before buying tools

Use invented data, your own materials, and tools you already have. Show the before-and-after difference. AI can suggest a structure or draft, but you need to check the output yourself.

Three examples to adapt

  • Patient explainer: a short lesson on a subject you know, with a practice exercise to check understanding.
  • Organized planner: a basic task tracker for an independent business, tested against its actual workflow.
  • Clear communicator: a rewritten instruction page that customers can follow without extra explanation.

Your first action

Spend 20 minutes creating a sample for one real problem. Ask someone in the intended audience to try it. Their response is more useful than a large feature list.
