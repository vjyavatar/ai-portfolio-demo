THE NEXTSTEP FIELD GUIDE

Use AI without giving away your judgment

AI can help prepare drafts, examples, and checklists. The person delivering the service still needs to understand the problem and verify the work.

Give AI a bounded task

Describe the audience, task, available time, and constraints. Ask for assumptions to be labeled. A request for three practice exercises on a defined topic is easier to review than a request to build a whole business.

Use a simple working prompt

Help me prepare one small service for [audience]: [deliverable]. I have [time] and can already [skills]. Suggest a sample, three questions to ask the customer, and a quality checklist. Label assumptions. Do not invent customer demand or earnings.

Keep the human responsibilities visible

  • Context: understand what the person actually needs and what they can use.
  • Accuracy: check claims, calculations, instructions, and code before delivery.
  • Permission: do not paste confidential material into an AI tool without authorization.
  • Accountability: agree on a result and check it with the recipient.

Test the output in the real task

Have a learner attempt the exercise. Ask a business owner to use the checklist. Run code against realistic failure cases. When a draft sounds confident, verify it with the same care as an uncertain one.

Start with one tool you already know

You can complete the NextStep experiment without using AI. If you use an assistant, introduce it where it reduces a specific effort you can measure. Avoid buying subscriptions until you understand the task and have a reason to need them.

What NextStep does

NextStep uses curated matching rules to suggest service ideas, makes a weekly plan, and helps you record the results. It does not run an autonomous agent or call an AI model. Its prompts are starting points for your own review.
